## Hello, cat. In what town does the cat reside?

The challenge requires identifying the town where the cat in the photograph is located.

While the foreground centers on a tabby cat facing away from the camera, the identifying geolocation artifacts reside in the surrounding heritage architecture. The background displays weathered ashlar limestone masonry typical of coastal Adriatic towns across Southern Europe. To the right beside the cat stands an ornate stone balustrade, while the adjacent palace wall presents multiple architectural anchors

![19. Pet the Cat.png](./images/19.%20Pet%20the%20Cat.png)

Isolating the building facade highlights four distinguishing structural details, consisting of three upper-story shuttered windows with dark timber blinds, a vintage carriage lantern affixed to the masonry corner, a dark wooden ground entrance door, and two small rectangular security windows flanking the doorway

![19. Pet the Cat-2.png](./images/19.%20Pet%20the%20Cat-2.png)

Submitting this cropped window and lantern layout to Google Lens returns direct photographic matches pinpointing the Drago Palace facade

![19. Pet the Cat-1.png](./images/19.%20Pet%20the%20Cat-1.png)

Consulting regional travel documentation from Lovin' Montenegro confirms that Drago Palace is a historic 14th to 16th-century noble residence located on Saint Tryphon's Square in the fortified Old Town of Kotor

![19. Pet the Cat-3.png](./images/19.%20Pet%20the%20Cat-3.png)

Navigating to Trg Sv. Tripuna in Kotor on Google Maps Street View verifies the complete vantage point in 360-degree imagery. On the left stands Drago Palace with its Gothic relief balcony and Montenegrin flag, while the stone balustrade adjacent to the cat corresponds to the ceremonial entryway steps of the Cathedral of Saint Tryphon
