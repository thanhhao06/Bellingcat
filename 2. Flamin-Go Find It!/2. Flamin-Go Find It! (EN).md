## What is the name of the area in which you'd find these flamingos?

Looking at the challenge photo, a flock of flamingos stands on a sandy bank beside a shallow pool with a curved concrete rim. Yellow identification bands on several birds' legs, along with the chain-link fence on the left and the wooden structure on the right, indicate an enclosure inside a zoo or wildlife park

To narrow down the global search, we first need to identify the exact flamingo species because zoos publish their species inventories in international registries. Zooming in on one bird shows its most distinctive trait in the leg pigmentation, where the pink ankle joint (which looks like a backward-bending knee) and pink webbed feet contrast with the pale gray-white leg shafts, paired with pale pink plumage and a light bill with a black tip

![2. Flamin-Go Find It!-1.png](./images/2.%20Flamin-Go%20Find%20It%21-1.png)

Comparing these physical traits against AnimalSpot's chart of all six flamingo species rules out solid-legged species such as the Greater Flamingo and American Flamingo. The match for pink joints and feet on pale gray legs is the Chilean Flamingo (*Phoenicopterus chilensis*)

![2. Flamin-Go Find It!.png](./images/2.%20Flamin-Go%20Find%20It%21.png)

Checking captive distribution records for *Phoenicopterus chilensis* from Zootierliste and the AZA shows roughly 67 zoos, aviaries, and wildlife parks across the United States housing this species. Using environmental cues in the photo, including the harsh sunlight casting sharp shadows, the sandy bank, and the dry grass tuft on the right, we start by checking the West Coast facilities first

![2. Flamin-Go Find It!-5.png](./images/2.%20Flamin-Go%20Find%20It%21-5.png)

Reviewing visitor photos on Google Maps for each West Coast zoo turns up a strong match at Woodland Park Zoo in Seattle, Washington, where the smooth curved concrete pool edge and the slope of the sandy bank closely resemble the challenge image

![2. Flamin-Go Find It!-4.png](./images/2.%20Flamin-Go%20Find%20It%21-4.png)

To verify the enclosure layout, we switch to a 360-degree Street View panorama taken right at the wooden viewing rail in front of the exhibit. From this angle, the view across the pool reveals a black-framed chain-link fence with a horizontal crossbar on the left and a dark flat-roofed wooden shelter right beside it

![2. Flamin-Go Find It!-2.png](./images/2.%20Flamin-Go%20Find%20It%21-2.png)

Comparing these two reference points back to the challenge photo confirms that both the crossbar on the left fence and the scalloped wooden shingle wall of the shelter on the right align in geometry and relative position
