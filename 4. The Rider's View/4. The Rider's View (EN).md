## The monument is to a person, what is their name?

The challenge requires identifying the historical figure sculpted into the bust monument depicted in an urban street view photograph.

Looking at the pedestrian street level perspective, the high-rise buildings in the background are generic and difficult to identify. The most distinct starting point is the black Chevrolet car driving right ahead

![4. The Rider's View-2.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View-2.png)

The license plate has a bright yellow background featuring 3 letters, a centered circular dot, 3 digits reading `INL-753`, and small capitalized text along the lower rim. Checking vehicle plate standards worldwide, this yellow 3-letter 3-digit format is characteristic of Colombia

![4. The Rider's View-1.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View-1.png)

Knowing the country is Colombia, we run a visual similarity search on Yandex using the surrounding streetscape, consisting of red brick high-rises, yellow cabs, and surrounding mountain ridges. The results show that this architectural atmosphere is quintessential to the capital city Bogotá

![4. The Rider's View.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View.png)

To narrow down the location within the city, we search for identifiable roadside objects. In the distance to the right, we spot the canopy of a fuel station colored in orange and white

![4. The Rider's View-10.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View-10.png)

However, searching for gas stations across Bogotá quickly hits a dead end because there are far too many scattered throughout the city, and checking them individually is not feasible since this station might not be registered or could have been removed from the map

![4. The Rider's View-11.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View-11.png)

Turning back to inspect the road surface, we notice a very unusual piece of transportation infrastructure, where the median separating opposing vehicular traffic lanes is actually a dedicated path for cyclists

![4. The Rider's View-12.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View-12.png)

Bogotá is globally celebrated for its extensive cycling network known as the Ciclorruta. We research which avenue in Bogotá has a Ciclorruta running along the central median

![4. The Rider's View-5.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View-5.png)

Transportation records show that the primary thoroughfare featuring a bike lane down the central median is Carrera Séptima, also known as Carrera 7

![4. The Rider's View-6.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View-6.png)

Having identified the main corridor, we focus on scanning along Carrera 7 on the map

![4. The Rider's View-7.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View-7.png)

We look for an intersection with a small park area or open green square positioned at the corner

![4. The Rider's View-9.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View-9.png)

Cross-referencing the corner with a park facing across from a gas station on the opposite side of the road, we locate Parque Mariscal Antonio José de Sucre, also called Parque de los Hippies, at the junction of Carrera 7 and Calle 60 in the Chapinero district

![4. The Rider's View-8.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View-8.png)

Dropping Street View at this corner facing the park, the entire scene from surrounding buildings to the distant gas station aligns. Noticeably, on the right sidewalk edge of the park sits a tiered stone pedestal holding a bronze bust

![4. The Rider's View-3.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View-3.png)

Cropping the image of the sculpture to search on Google Images

![4. The Rider's View-4.png](./images/4.%20The%20Rider%27s%20View-4.png)

The photograph is archived under the title Busto de Julio Flórez en el Parque Publico Sucre Hippies. The monument commemorates Julio Flórez, a renowned Colombian romantic poet who lived from 1867 to 1923
