## What is the ICAO code for the airport?

The challenge photo shows a flat stretch of grassland under dense fog with the faint outline of an aircraft frame in the distance, leaving no signs or terrain landmarks for visual comparison. With visual cues too limited to identify the location, the first step is inspecting the technical metadata embedded in the original image file

Inspecting the JPEG file reveals an intact EXIF header using `Big-endian (Motorola, MM)` byte order. Specifically, tags inside the `GPSInfo` IFD (version `2.3.0.0`) record geographic coordinates directly from the camera at `4032x3024` resolution, listing `GPS Latitude` as `51 deg 10' 13.08" N`, `GPS Longitude` as `4 deg 40' 0.12" W`, and `131 m Above Sea Level`
![5. Lost in the Fog-3.png](./images/5.%20Lost%20in%20the%20Fog-3.png)

Converting the coordinates from degrees, minutes, and seconds (DMS) into decimal form yields `51.170306, -4.666694`, which we plot on Google Maps to locate the site on satellite imagery. The pin drops on the southern end of Lundy Island (`Lundy Heritage Coast`, near `National Trust - Lundy`, Plus Code `58CM+48F Bideford`), a small UK island in the Bristol Channel
![5. Lost in the Fog-1.png](./images/5.%20Lost%20in%20the%20Fog-1.png)

Combining the aircraft silhouette in the challenge photo with this location, we search for aviation facilities on Lundy Island. The official information page for **Lundy Island Airfield** confirms a small grassy airfield next to the Old Light with published coordinates at `51.1705, -4.6708`, closely matching the EXIF GPS point and the grassy terrain in the photo. Under Airfield Details, the airfield carries the ICAO identifier `EGZV`, following the `EG` prefix used for airfields in the United Kingdom
